Our mission · How we work

Open science, as a discipline

Est. Texas

Good science is science other people can check. So we publish our code, our methods, and our results — including the results that come out negative — and we build our experiments so that anyone with the skill can run them again and get the same answer. When we're wrong, the record shows it, and the work improves in public.

Transparent research

Methods, data sources, and limitations are stated up front, in plain language. If a result is a prototype result, it is labeled a prototype result. If a baseline beats our own model, we say so — in the headline, not the footnotes.

Reproducible experimentation

Experiments ship with the code and data needed to re-run them. Our combustion research, for example, is built entirely on published NASA measurements, with anything synthetic kept separate and clearly labeled.

Interdisciplinary collaboration

The interesting problems sit between fields — materials and computation, networks and mathematics, cognition and machines. We actively seek collaborators in universities, labs, and independent practice.

Knowledge for the public good

Every repository we own is licensed under the GNU AGPL-3.0. Anyone may study it, run it, and build on it — regardless of who they are or what they can pay.

Research program · Computational architectures

Sentinel & the HRM Architectures

Active research

AI systems quietly change over time: their answers drift, wander off-topic, or start making things up, and nobody notices until the damage is done. Our Sentinel research builds a "smoke alarm" for that drift — a system that watches every answer an AI gives, learns what good looks like, and can say, for each answer: let it through, steady it, try again, switch to a backup, or hand it to a person. Underneath it sit our HRM architectures — hierarchical reasoning models we are developing to match simpler systems' accuracy with less computation, and to stay robust when the incoming data is noisy or damaged. The honest status: a viable research platform with demonstrated efficiency and robustness results — not yet a validated superior architecture, and we publish it that way.

Research program · Network science

An Oracle View of
Computer Networks

Working prototype

Big computer networks — the kind that move data around the world — are hard to watch. By the time a slowdown shows up on a dashboard, people are already feeling it. Our Syzygy research keeps two pictures of the network at once: one of what's happening right now, and one of what's likely to happen half a second from now. When the two stop matching, that's an early warning — congestion building, hardware overheating, traffic heading the wrong way — while there's still time to fix it. Every change it records has to explain itself: what happened, what caused it, and how the numbers add up. Nothing is a black box.

Research program · Emerging technologies

IFT Quantum Experiments

Live laboratory

Our IFT (Information Field Theory) program tests a simple, checkable idea: whether carefully restructuring a quantum circuit can remove operations a real machine would otherwise have to perform — and whether what remains still computes the same answer. The experiments run on real quantum-computing infrastructure (Amazon Braket), comparing noisy and ideal runs side by side. In our published sweeps, the restructuring removed every targeted two-qubit operation and measurably improved the fidelity of the result on simulated noisy hardware. The lab is open: you can visit it, read the compiler research, and see exactly where the method wins, where it ties, and where it doesn't.

Research program · Foundational frameworks

FlameScope — Combustion in Freefall

Open experiment

How a fuel droplet burns in orbit — where there is no "up" for a flame to lean toward — turns out to matter for spacecraft fire safety. FlameScope is our open research platform for NASA's published microgravity combustion measurements: 276 real droplet experiments from the International Space Station, explored on an interactive manifold, with our HRM graph model compared honestly against a simple statistical baseline. On held-out tests, the simple baseline currently wins — and the platform says so, in the comparison view, because that's what the data shows. Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge.

Research program · Materials for energy

Bio-Inspired Materials for Energy

Phase 1 planned

Our Harmonium SetSilk research asks whether a "smart skin" for energy devices — a thin, graded material patch — can report on its own condition: swelling in a battery pouch cell, heat in power electronics, wear in a flexible storage device. The program is deliberately strict with itself: three-way controlled comparisons, thresholds written down before the experiments run, and honest go/no-go decisions at each stage. It is our entry in the TeraOpenScience "Idea, Innovation, Business" Science Challenge, and we are actively scheduling laboratory time and recruiting collaborators in materials science, electrochemistry, and battery engineering — including outreach to UT Austin, Texas A&M, Rice, the University of Houston, UT Dallas, Texas A&M–Corpus Christi, and Texas A&M–Kingsville.

Research program · Cognition & edge AI

Quadra — Minds on Machines

Research systems

Most AI lives in a data center and needs the internet to think. The Quadra program explores the opposite: AI that runs on the device itself — a personal machine, a drone, a sensor — privately, offline, and under its owner's control. It includes Quadra-M.I.N.D. Sight, our rounds-based cognition application; Quadra-Seer Sovereign, a local-first AI system with persistent memory that is now free and open source for anyone, regardless of means; and Quadra Kestrel, research into brain-inspired AI small enough to sit inside a drone's flight controller and react in a split second, with no connection at all.

Learns like a brain, runs on a chip

Kestrel's AI is modeled on real brain cells, so it learns from experience instead of following one fixed script — and it can also follow plain written rules, so its decisions stay sensible.

Tested hard before it flies

Training tools, live dashboards, and step-by-step test guides exist to find the AI's mistakes in the lab — not in the air. Every stage has its own tests and written summaries.

Applied programs · Research in the field

Watching the World Change

Running weekly

Two of our programs take the research out of the lab and put it to work in public. Their service revenue helps fund the open research above — and their findings are published for anyone to read.

AI Change Radar

An observatory for the AI industry itself: Radar watches dozens of AI vendors' prices, features, and rules, and publishes a weekly brief of what changed and what it means. Businesses subscribe for a personalized edition; the public brief is free to read.

Visit aichangeradar.com

Cryptocurrency Augury Prism

A market-research instrument that weighs seven independent signals against live crypto market data and writes up what it finds in plain English — with an honest scorekeeper that tests its own approach on real historical prices, costs included, and shows the losses as plainly as the wins. A research tool, not financial advice.

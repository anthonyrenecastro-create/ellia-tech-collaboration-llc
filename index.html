Our mission · How we work

Science you can check

Est. Texas

Good science is science other people can check. So we publish our methods and our results — including the results that come out negative — and we build our experiments so that anyone with the skill can run them again and get the same answer. When we're wrong, the record shows it, and the work improves in public.

Transparent research

Methods, data sources, and limitations are stated up front, in plain language. If a result is a prototype result, it is labeled a prototype result. If a baseline beats our own model, we say so — in the headline, not the footnotes.

Reproducible experimentation

Experiments ship with the methods and data needed to re-run them, with anything synthetic kept separate and clearly labeled.

Interdisciplinary collaboration

The interesting problems sit between fields — materials and computation, networks and mathematics, cognition and machines. We actively seek collaborators in universities, labs, and independent practice.

Knowledge for the public good

We publish our findings and state our methods and limitations in plain language, so independent researchers can check the work — regardless of who they are or what they can pay.

Research track · Materials for energy

Harmonium Research Track

Phase 1 planned

Our Harmonium SetSilk research asks whether a "smart skin" for energy devices — a thin, graded material patch — can report on its own condition: swelling in a battery pouch cell, heat in power electronics, wear in a flexible storage device. The reference design is the SetSilk Bio-C architecture: a 10 cm × 10 cm sensing patch with 16–64 sensing regions, built from bio-inspired gradient composites with distributed tactile sensing and state memory. The program is deliberately strict with itself: three-way controlled comparisons, thresholds written down before the experiments run, and honest go/no-go decisions at each stage. It is our entry in the TeraOpenScience "Idea, Innovation, Business" Science Challenge, and we are actively scheduling laboratory time and recruiting collaborators in materials science, electrochemistry, and battery engineering — including outreach to UT Austin, Texas A&M, Rice, the University of Houston, UT Dallas, Texas A&M–Corpus Christi, and Texas A&M–Kingsville.

Research track · Multiverse cosmology

Multi Modal Recursive Multiverse Research Track

Open questions

The Multi Modal Recursive Multiverse (MMURM) program is a derivation layer: it asks what a multiverse built from recursive, multi-modal relationships must look like — and what follows for topology, spectra, and cosmology. The framework admits exactly two candidate branch counts, N = 5 and N = 12, and the honest status is that no branch is pinned down. A minimization criterion favors N = 5 on reported values; the historical test count favors N = 12, though the audit has discounted much of that weight. What would settle it is still open: a definition of the F functional, a resolution of the Hawking-slope question, and a real dataset with a comparative statistic. The program's simulated results are published as construction and simulation, not canon — simulated data are not observations, and this track says so.

Phase Recursion Ontology Research Track

Published monograph

Phase Recursion Ontology is the foundational framework behind much of this work: an ontology of recursion — how systems fold back on themselves, phase by phase, and what that folding explains about consciousness, cosmology, and computation. Research track · Cognition & edge AI

QuadraSeer Research Track

Research systems

Most AI lives in a data center and needs the internet to think. The QuadraSeer track explores the opposite: AI that runs on the device itself — a personal machine, a drone, a sensor — privately, offline, and under its owner's control. Its centerpiece is Quadra-Seer Sovereign, a local-first reasoning system with persistent memory, built to be inspected: balanced, auditable, and sovereign by design. Alongside it, Quadra-M.I.N.D. Sight brings rounds-based cognition to a personal device, and Quadra Kestrel researches brain-inspired AI small enough to sit inside a drone's flight controller and react in a split second, with no connection at all.

Learns like a brain, runs on a chip

Kestrel's AI is modeled on real brain cells, so it learns from experience instead of following one fixed script — and it can also follow plain written rules, so its decisions stay sensible.

Tested hard before it flies

Training tools, live dashboards, and step-by-step test guides exist to find the AI's mistakes in the lab — not in the air. Every stage has its own tests and written summaries.
